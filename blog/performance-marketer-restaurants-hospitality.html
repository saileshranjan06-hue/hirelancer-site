Freelance Performance Marketing for Restaurants and Hospitality Brands

By Hirelancer.agency Team · Published August 21, 2026 · 7 min read
Quick answer: Hire a freelance performance marketer with real local-business experience, not a generalist. Look for someone comfortable with hyper-local geo-targeting, tracking both footfall and online orders, and planning around seasonal or festival demand spikes. Expect to pay roughly ₹20,000 to ₹80,000+ per month depending on how many locations and channels are involved.

Restaurants and hospitality brands don't sell through a website checkout the way an e-commerce store does.

Success shows up as a table booked, a delivery order placed, or a room reserved, often within a few kilometers of the ad itself. A marketer used to national e-commerce campaigns can genuinely struggle here.

This guide covers what's different about hiring for this sector, and what to check before you bring someone on.

Why local, hyper-targeted campaigns matter most

Most restaurant and hospitality demand comes from a tight radius around the physical location.

  • Geofenced targeting. Ads shown to people within a few kilometers, or near competing venues, rather than a broad city-wide audience.
  • Delivery-platform promotions. Coordinating paid ads with promotions on Zomato, Swiggy, or similar platforms rather than running paid ads in isolation.
  • Multi-location coordination. For chains or franchises, each location often needs its own local targeting rather than one blanket campaign.

A generalist marketer without local-business experience will often run one broad campaign and miss all three.

What a good hospitality marketer actually does

Day to day, the work centers on a few things:

  • Running geofenced Meta and Google campaigns targeted around the venue's location
  • Tracking both footfall (via reservations or in-store visits) and online order volume, not just clicks
  • Planning spend around seasonal spikes, like festivals, wedding season, or local events
  • Coordinating with delivery-platform promotions so paid ads and platform placement work together, not against each other

For a broader sense of what the role covers generally, see our guide to what a performance marketer actually does.

Reviews and reputation affect ad performance too

This is a detail generalist marketers often miss.

A dip in Google review ratings, or a run of recent negative reviews, can quietly raise ad costs and lower conversion rates, since ad platforms and searchers both weigh reputation signals.

An experienced hospitality marketer will flag a reputation problem rather than just keep spending against it. Ask a candidate directly whether they track review ratings alongside ad performance.

What to check before you hire

  • Ask for a past example of a geofenced or radius-targeted campaign they've run, and what radius they used and why.
  • Ask how they've coordinated paid ads with delivery-platform promotions, if relevant to your business.
  • Ask how they track footfall or reservations, not just online clicks.
  • Ask whether they've handled multi-location campaigns, if you run more than one venue.

What a realistic budget looks like

Freelance performance marketers with restaurant or hospitality experience typically charge ₹20,000 to ₹80,000+ per month for their management fee, separate from ad spend. This is in line with the broader ranges in our freelance performance marketer cost guide.

A single-location restaurant with a modest local budget typically sits toward the lower end. A multi-location chain running coordinated campaigns across several venues sits toward the higher end.

Campaigns are hyper-local rather than broad, success is measured in footfall and online orders rather than just clicks, and demand shifts sharply around seasons, festivals, and local events.
